## Executive Summary

Jabil Inc. (NYSE: JBL), headquartered in St. Petersburg, Florida, stands at a critical juncture in its corporate transformation as of September 30, 2026. Following the multi-billion-dollar divestment of its lower-margin Mobility consumer electronics business to BYD Electronic, Jabil has reallocated its industrial footprint toward high-density **Intelligent Infrastructure**. This segment—encompassing AI data center hardware, high-density server rack integration, liquid cooling distribution units, and advanced power systems—has emerged as the primary growth engine, with full-year Fiscal Year 2026 AI-related revenue expectations standing at **$13.1 billion to $13.6 billion** out of total enterprise revenues of approximately **$35.0 billion**.

While capital markets present a highly bullish consensus (Wall Street price targets ranging between $375 and $441 per share versus current trading levels around $310 to $319), institutional analysis reveals a **structural divergence between reported core non-GAAP operating results and underlying GAAP economic reality**. 

For the final quarter of Fiscal Year 2026, a **$2.02 per share gap** exists between management’s Non-GAAP Core EPS target ($4.07) and true GAAP Diluted EPS ($2.05). This variance stems from uncapitalized operational disruption expenses at East Coast facilities (St. Petersburg, FL, and North Carolina), ongoing footprint rationalization charges under its multi-year Restructuring Plan, and stock-based compensation overhead. Furthermore, physical capacity audits indicate that output velocity is constrained not by real estate footprint, but by **megawatt utility grid power drops and thermal burn-in testing bottlenecks**, applying an estimated **12.6% efficiency haircut** to near-term conversion capacity.

Supported by a **$1.5 billion share repurchase authorization** initiated in July 2026—bringing cumulative buybacks and dividends near $8 billion since 2016—management continues to shrink weighted average shares outstanding to support per-share metrics. Institutional capital must evaluate Jabil by weighing its undisputed position in AI compute infrastructure against near-term operational retooling friction and accounting reconciliation risks.

## Forensic Accounting Audit

### Standards Application Framework
Jabil Inc. reports under **US GAAP (FASB ASC)**. Given its complex cross-border supply chain (including APAC manufacturing nodes in Penang, Malaysia, and asset transfers involving entities listed in Hong Kong like BYD Electronic), rigorous evaluation of cross-border accounting policies is mandatory.

* **Mezzanine Capital & Hybrid Instruments (ASC 480 / ASC 815-40):** The $1.5 billion share repurchase authorization requires tracking of forward purchase commitments and settlement mechanics to ensure proper classification as equity versus liability obligations. Joint venture structures supporting regional growth containing holder redemption options must be segregated into Mezzanine Equity under ASC 480-10-S99.
* **Capitalized Interest Standards (ASC 835-20):** Interest capitalization applies to qualifying ongoing facility builds (Marshall County, TN, and Penang, Malaysia). Capitalization must cease immediately upon physical completion; continuing to capitalize borrowing costs during commercial ramp-ups violates ASC 835-20 and artificially inflates core net income.
* **Property, Plant, and Equipment & Disruption Accounting (ASC 360 / ASC 450-30):** Operating disruptions across St. Petersburg, FL, and North Carolina sites demand strict bifurcation. Idle facility costs and damaged equipment must be expensed as period losses under ASC 360/ASC 330, while anticipated insurance recoveries cannot offset losses until gain contingencies are fully realized under ASC 450-30.

#### Detailed Breakdown of Physical Choke Points

1. **Megawatt Power Drops & Thermal Burn-In Infrastructure (Primary Bottleneck):**  
   Modern AI compute server racks draw between **80 kW and 120+ kW per rack**. A standard advanced manufacturing plant with a dedicated 20 Megawatt utility drop can allocate roughly 40% of its total power (8 Megawatts) directly to the active burn-in testing floor. Assuming a required 48-hour continuous thermal stress test per rack, the site faces an absolute physical limitation of testing approximately **80 concurrent racks**, yielding a maximum ceiling of **280 fully tested AI server racks per week per site**. Adding power capacity requires utility grid approvals and transformer lead times that often extend 12 to 24 months.

2. **Upstream Material & Memory Component Allocation:**  
   Supporting high-density compute architectures requires massive upfront procurement of High-Bandwidth Memory (HBM) and power supply units, as demonstrated by the **$1.7 billion memory authorization tied to Akamai and AI compute clients**. A delay in a single auxiliary component (such as liquid cooling cold plates) stalls the entire assembly, converting floor space into static Work-In-Progress inventory.

3. **Floor Slab Structural Reinforcement:**  
   Fully populated 42U/48U liquid-cooled racks weigh up to 3,500 lbs. Converting legacy consumer electronics facilities into rack assembly centers requires structural slab reinforcement (pouring thick fiber-reinforced concrete) and installing heavy gantry cranes. Facilities undergoing conversion experience temporary shop-floor utilization drops.

4. **Multi-Echelon Freight Logistics:**  
   Sub-assemblies flow out of Penang, Malaysia, crossing the Pacific to integration sites in North America. Regional operational disruptions, such as the site disruptions experienced in St. Petersburg, Florida and North Carolina, disrupt domestic transit corridors and cause staging delays for final delivery to enterprise data center sites.
