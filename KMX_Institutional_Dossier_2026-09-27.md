## Executive Summary

CarMax, Inc. (NYSE: KMX) stands at a pivotal operational and valuation inflection point as it reports its second-quarter Fiscal Year 2027 earnings on September 29, 2026. Operating as the primary omnichannel retailer and wholesaler of used vehicles in the United States, the company's ecosystem encompasses retail stores, dealer-to-dealer auctions, CarMax Auto Finance (CAF), MaxCare extended protection plans, and the Edmunds.com platform.

Following executive transitions in early 2026, President and CEO Keith Barr launched a four-pillar operational strategy focused on lean operations, digital customer interface enhancements, unit reconditioning efficiency, and volume growth. Central to this strategy is an aggressive overhead reduction program targeting **$200 million in annualized cost savings by Fiscal Year 2027**. To achieve this objective, management executed three distinct corporate workforce reductions within an 11-month window:
* **October 2025:** Elimination of ~350 positions across Customer Experience Centers.
* **January 2026:** Reduction of ~230 roles across the Richmond Home Office and CAF finance divisions.
* **September 18–21, 2026:** Cutting of 145 corporate roles (~4% of the corporate workforce), concentrated in technology (~41% of cuts), product, accounting, marketing, and Edmunds.

The capital markets maintain a cautious, Neutral/Equal-Weight stance on KMX (consensus target $54.85–$58.00), modeling $0.70 EPS on $6.94 Billion in revenue for Q2 FY2027. Wall Street's consensus assumes that persistent high consumer interest rates and affordability constraints will compress gross margins and cancel out SG&A savings. 

Our institutional evaluation reveals a major mispricing: Wall Street has failed to account for the velocity of CarMax's operating leverage drop-through and the specific timing rules of U.S. GAAP accounting governing workforce restructurings. Under **ASC 855 (Subsequent Events)**, the September 18–21 layoff wave constitutes a Type 2 Non-Recognized Subsequent Event relative to the August 31, 2026 quarterly close. Consequently, Q2 FY2027 reported earnings will absorb zero severance costs from the September wave (~$8.5 million pre-tax charge pushed to Q3 FY2027) while simultaneously capturing the run-rate operational benefits of the prior two restructuring rounds. We project a **Q2 FY2027 EPS beat of $1.02 versus $0.70 consensus**, creating an asymmetric re-rating catalyst on print morning.

### ASC 855 Subsequent Events Audit of Workforce Reductions
The reporting cutoff date for Q2 FY2027 is **August 31, 2026**. Under **ASC 855**, events between August 31 and the September 29 filing date are categorized as follows:
* **Wave 1 (October 2025 - ~350 roles):** Settled in prior periods; fully reflected in baseline SG&A run-rate.
* **Wave 2 (January 2026 - ~230 roles):** Settled in prior periods; fully reflected in baseline SG&A run-rate.
* **Wave 3 (September 18–21, 2026 - 145 roles):** Classified as a **Type 2 Non-Recognized Subsequent Event**. Under **ASC 420**, liabilities for exit and disposal costs are recognized when communicated to employees post-period. Consequently, zero severance liabilities or restructuring charges (~$8.5 million estimated pre-tax) can be accrued in the Q2 FY2027 balance sheet. Financial statements require footnote disclosure only.

### Physical Capacity Limit Models

1. **Reconditioning Throughput Ceiling:** Across ~6,500 active reconditioning bays nationwide operating 2,500 hours annually at 78% utilization and an average repair cycle time of 10.5 labor hours per vehicle, the theoretical maximum output capacity is **1.21 Million units per year**. If cycle times expand to 13.0 hours due to lean staffing, annual output capacity drops by 19.2% to **975,000 units**.
2. **Yard Staging Capacity Limits:** Total nationwide capacity across 245+ locations sits at ~185,000 parking stalls. At an 85% operational density threshold to prevent lot gridlock:
   * *At 60 Days Sales of Inventory (DSI):* Maximum yard throughput capacity drops to **956,000 units per year**.
   * *At 42 Days Sales of Inventory (DSI):* Operational yard throughput expands to **1.37 Million units per year**.
3. **Effective System Ceiling:** CarMax’s operational ceiling is currently constrained at **910,000 units per year**. The governing choke point is not physical real estate or raw reconditioning bays, but **administrative delivery friction** following Customer Experience Center workforce cuts and elevated inventory holding periods (DSI > 58 days).
