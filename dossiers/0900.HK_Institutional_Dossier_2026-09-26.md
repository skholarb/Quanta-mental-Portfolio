## Executive Summary

AEON Credit Service (Asia) Company Limited (HKEX: 0900 / 00900) operates as a specialized consumer finance institution headquartered in Tsim Sha Tsui, Kowloon, Hong Kong. As a key subsidiary of Japan’s AEON Financial Service Co., Ltd. and the broader AEON Group, the Company derives its primary revenues from consumer credit card issuance, merchant acquiring, personal unsecured loan financing, insurance brokerage, and targeted microfinance operations in Mainland China.

As of September 28, 2026, the Company has successfully resolved its primary regulatory overhang. In August 2026, the entity restored its public float to 29.57%, fully satisfying Hong Kong Stock Exchange (HKEX) Rule 8.08(1)(a) compliance following shareholding re-allocations between parent entities and institutional investors (notably FMR LLC). Operating with a stable workforce of over 600 employees, AEON Credit has accelerated its digital transformation through the "AEON HK" mobile platform and a unified bonus point integration with affiliate AEON Stores (Hong Kong) Co., Limited.

With the stock trading at HK$ 9.00–9.10 per share (~8.0x P/E ratio), the market prices AEON Credit as a static, defensive income asset yielding 6.4%–6.7%. However, institutional analysis indicates a clear divergence: the resolution of public float friction combined with digital transaction velocity and automated credit scoring positions the firm to re-rate toward its intrinsic discounted cash flow (DCF) fair value of HK$ 11.40 per share.

## Forensic Accounting Audit

### 1. Accounting Standards Framework & Governing Rules
AEON Credit prepares its consolidated financial statements under **Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS)**, fully converged with **IFRS**. Group reporting aligns with **J-GAAP** for Japanese parent consolidation, while mainland China operations comply with **PRC GAAP**.

* **HKAS 32 / HKFRS 9 (Capital Structure):** The capital structure consists of standard bank credit facilities, unsecured senior debt, and collateralized obligations. The firm carries zero hybrid mezzanine debt or perpetual equity instruments, preventing hidden dividend drains or artificial equity inflation.
* **HKAS 23 (Borrowing Cost Cutoffs):** Capitalization of interest expenses related to internal IT infrastructure (such as the upgraded "AEON HK" digital gateway) was formally terminated upon operational deployment in early 2026. Ongoing financing costs are fully recognized in profit or loss.
* **HKAS 16 / HKAS 38 (Digital Asset Amortization):** Software and application development costs are capitalized under intangible assets and amortized on a straight-line basis over 3 to 5 years starting immediately upon go-live. Audit checks confirm no deferred expenses remain trapped in work-in-progress (WIP) accounts.
* **HKAS 21 (FX Remeasurement):** Operating assets are denominated in HKD. Mainland microfinance assets in RMB are translated at period-end spot rates with translation adjustments routed to Other Comprehensive Income (OCI). Credit loss provisions on RMB assets are charged directly to consolidated profit or loss under HKFRS 9.

### 4. Key Forensic Audit Red Flags

1. **HKFRS 9 Loan Staging in Mainland Microfinance:** Unsecured micro-loans in China carry heightened risk of rolling from Stage 2 (underperforming) to Stage 3 (default). 2. **HKFRS 15 Contract Liabilities (AEON Bonus Point Program):** Unredeemed customer loyalty points represent deferred revenue contract liabilities. Audit checks monitor breakage rates to ensure deferred income isn’t recognized prematurely to boost short-term operating profit.
3. **HKAS 38 Intangible Amortization Drag:** Capitalized technology expenditures for the AEON HK Mobile App create a fixed non-cash amortization expense over 3–5 years. Fee income growth must outpace this fixed amortization drag to ensure expanding operating margins.

### 2. Theoretical Operational Capacity Ceilings

#### A. Digital Transaction & Payment Gateway Capacity
* **Core Processing Ceiling:** The enterprise transaction ledger is architected to process a peak capability of **1,200 Transactions Per Second (TPS)**.
* **Daily Operational Limit:** Operating with a 30% safety headroom to protect against latency spikes during peak retail events, the system maintains an operational ceiling of **~31.1 Million transactions per day**.

#### B. Credit Application Underwriting Capacity
* **Workforce Split:** Out of ~600 total staff, approximately 30% (**180 FTEs**) are assigned to credit underwriting, exception handling, and KYC/AML reviews.
* **Automated vs. Manual Pipeline:**
  * **Straight-Through Processing (STP) Rate:** ~85% of standard applications are processed instantly by automated scoring models.
  * **Manual Exception Rate:** ~15% require human manual review (12 minutes per review per FTE).
  * **Manual Processing Ceiling:** 180 FTEs operating at 40 reviews/day yield a capacity ceiling of **7,200 manual exception reviews per day**.
  * **Systemic Ingestion Ceiling:** Based on a 15% exception rate, the maximum daily intake capacity across digital and physical channels is **48,000 credit applications per day**.

#### C. Physical Card Manufacturing & Distribution Logistics
* **Embossing Capability:** Contracted personalization machinery can produce up to 38,400 physical cards daily across two production lines.
* **Postal Injection Bottleneck:** Local secure mail injection limits (Hongkong Post and courier limits) restrict distribution to **15,000 physical cards delivered per day** without triggering expedited shipping surcharges.
