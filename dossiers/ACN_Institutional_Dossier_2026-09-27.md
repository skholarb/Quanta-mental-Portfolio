## Supply Chain & Output Ceiling

In enterprise professional services, the supply chain coordinates billable talent, delivery center networks, hyperscaler infrastructure allocations, and proprietary platforms to generate client output.

### Global Delivery Network (GDN) Architecture
* **Offshore Hubs (60%–65% Volume):** India (Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Chennai) and the Philippines (Manila). These hubs deliver scalable, low-cost engineering at $25 to $40 per hour fully loaded.
* **Nearshore Hubs (15%–20% Volume):** Latin America and Eastern Europe ($60 to $90 per hour), providing real-time time-zone alignment for core clients.
* **Onshore Hubs (20% Volume):** Client-embedded teams and specialized innovation centers ($160 to $250+ per hour).

### Output Ceiling Analysis
Accenture’s capacity limits depend on whether operations rely purely on linear billable hours or asset-augmented delivery models.

2. **Hyperscaler Compute & API Bottlenecks:** Enterprise GenAI rollouts depend on compute node allocations and model API rate limits from partners like AWS and Anthropic. Compute provisioning bottlenecks create a 60-to-120-day conversion lag between initial contract signing and recognized revenue.
3. **Offshore Advanced Technology Center (ATC) Attrition Lead-Time:** In high-demand offshore regions (India), replacing specialized talent requires up to 180 total days (sourcing, notice periods, and productivity ramping). High attrition forces the deployment of higher-cost onshore staff, reducing contract gross margins by up to 1,200 basis points.
4. **OT/Cybersecurity Integration Overhead:** Integrating specialized software tools from Dragos, runZero, and NetRise into Industry X frameworks involves complex security clearance and sovereign data compliance protocols, delaying full cross-selling margin benefits by 6 to 9 months post-acquisition.
