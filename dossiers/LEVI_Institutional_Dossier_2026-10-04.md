## Executive Summary

**Levi Strauss & Co. (NYSE: LEVI)** is executing a multi-year structural pivot to become a "Brand-Led, Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) First" global market leader. Headquartered in San Francisco, California, the company operates across the Americas, Europe, and Asia, anchored by its core flagship Levi’s® brand, high-growth activewear vehicle Beyond Yoga®, and mass-market value lines Signature by Levi Strauss & Co.™ and Denizen®. 

As of **October 07, 2026**, the corporate strategy is defined by aggressive portfolio sharpening, direct distribution scaling, and fixed-overhead reduction:

* **Direct-to-Consumer Elevation:** DTC sales (e-commerce and company-operated retail stores) now represent over 50% of global net revenues, reducing reliance on legacy wholesale channels and enhancing gross margin expansion.
* **Supply Chain Modernization & Cost Structural Shift:** Under **Project FUEL**—a multi-year global productivity initiative—LEVI has shuttered its company-owned distribution facility in Hebron, Kentucky (resulting in 303 facility layoffs in late August 2026). Global logistics operations are permanently transitioning toward a hybrid 3PL (third-party logistics) model powered by Maersk (inbound freight/ocean consolidation) and GXO Logistics (domestic fulfillment node management).
* **Portfolio Rationalization:** Management has placed the non-core global **Dockers®** brand under active strategic review for divestment, carving out non-performing assets to reallocate capital into high-margin denim categories and expanding activewear footprints.
* **Executive Leadership Realignment:** Following CEO Michelle Gass’s strategic realignment, Chief Financial Officer Harmit Singh is transitioning out, with John Vandemore appointed Executive Vice President and CFO, effective November 1, 2026.
* **Near-Term Financial Baseline:** For Q3 FY2026 (reporting after market close on October 07, 2026), consensus market expectations target **$1.62 Billion** in Net Revenue (~4.5% YoY growth) and an **Adjusted EPS of $0.36**. Full-year FY2026 revenue growth guidance remains set at 7.0%–7.5%, with Adjusted Diluted EPS guided between $1.46 and $1.52.

### System Network Throughput Capacity

To support the full-year net revenue target ($6.5B–$6.8B), the global network's physical volume requirements operate under the following parameters:

* **Financial Revenue Baseline:** ~$6.60 Billion annual run-rate.
* **Blended Average Unit Realization (AUR):** ~$26.50 per unit across DTC and Wholesale.
* **Annual System Volume Requirement:** ~249 Million Units per year.
* **Peak Volume Velocity (Q4 Peak Demand Factor ~1.4x):** ~87 Million Units per quarter (~965,000 Units per day globally).

### Critical Physical & Operational Choke Points

1. **Inbound Maritime Consolidation Gateways:** Sole-partner reliance on Maersk for ocean freight consolidation creates a single-point operational node. Buffer stock reductions under Project FUEL leave retail store inventory with less than 14 days of time-to-out-of-stock buffer if port dwell times spike.
2. **Post-Hebron Regional Fulfillment Capacity:** Shutting down the Hebron node right before peak seasonal volume building (Q3/Q4) creates short-term operational risk. Volume transferred to GXO multi-tenant nodes relies on rapid system integration between LEVI's ERP and GXO’s warehouse management system, alongside localized labor scaling.
3. **DTC Piece-Pick vs. Wholesale Case-Pack Velocity:** Wholesale distribution processes case-packs at high rates (800–1,200 units per labor hour). Crossing the 50% DTC threshold forces item-level piece-picking, dropping operator output to 80–150 units per labor hour without automated unit-sorting infrastructure. Peak period performance depends on GXO’s parcel sorting capacity exceeding 300,000 discrete orders per day globally.
4. **Dockers® Operational Decoupling Friction:** Physical and digital segregation of Dockers® inventory from core Levi’s® stock within shared 3PL footprints introduces temporary node friction. Re-labeling, slotting adjustments, and ERP data separation can reduce warehouse throughput efficiency by 5% to 12% during active carve-out phases.
