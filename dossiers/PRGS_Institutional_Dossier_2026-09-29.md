Because the $400 million Domo asset acquisition closed after the Q3 fiscal cutoff, Q3 financial performance reflects pure post-synergy operational margins without transaction friction, interest expense drag, or balance sheet leverage from the subsequent credit facility draw.

### Balance Sheet & Accounting Red Flags

1. **Unearned Revenue Haircut Dynamics (ASC 606):** Under ASU 2021-08, acquired unearned revenue is recorded based on performance obligations rather than exit-value haircuts. Rapid sequential acquisitions carry the risk of masking organic ARR attrition through unearned revenue runoff.
2. **Benchmark Rate Exposure on Credit Draws:** Funding $400 million in cash for Domo via revolving debt increases leverage to approximately 3.8x Net Debt to EBITDA or higher. Variable borrowing rates under benchmark rates will create higher floating interest expense beginning in Q4 FY2026.
3. **Internal Software Capitalization Boundaries (ASC 350-40):** Capitalizing internal-use software development costs during post-merger integration can artificially depress operational expenses and inflate non-GAAP operating margins. Auditing capitalization levels against historical baselines is critical to verify Free Cash Flow conversion quality.

### Theoretical Operational Ceilings & Bottlenecks

#### 1. Cloud Infrastructure & Hosting Ceiling (The Domo Infrastructure Floor)
With the acquisition of Domo, Progress absorbs an AI and data visualization platform running intensive ETL pipelines and real-time query executions across ~2,400 enterprise business accounts.

* **Target Operating Margin Envelope:** Non-GAAP Operating Margin targets of 38%–41% require COGS to remain below 18%–22% of revenue. On a $247.0 million quarterly base, total hosting and infrastructure COGS cannot exceed **$54.3 million**.
* **Data Logistics Capacity Ceiling:** Assuming Domo’s ~2,400 enterprise accounts ingest and query an average of 1.5 Terabytes per day per client, baseline system throughput equals **3.6 Petabytes per day**.
* **Operational Ceiling:** Standard enterprise cloud hosting costs (~$0.05/GB for compute and egress overhead) set Domo’s baseline infrastructure cost at ~$16.2 million per quarter. If client query volume or AI inference workloads drive total enterprise data ingestion past **4.8 Petabytes per day**, compute costs will exceed the $22.0 million allocation limit for Domo, compressing non-GAAP operating margins below the 38% target floor.

#### 2. Engineering Maintenance Throughput Ceiling
Following post-acquisition headcount restructuring (~200 positions trimmed from ShareFile), core engineering staffing stands at ~1,100 FTEs across 7 core product lines (OpenEdge, Telerik, Sitefinity, Chef, ShareFile, DataDirect, Domo).

* **Engineering Time Allocation:** 1,100 engineering FTEs provide ~154,000 productive development hours per month (~157 FTEs per product line).
* **Maintenance & Security Capacity:** Standard codebase maintenance, vulnerability patching, and infrastructure updates absorb ~65% of dev capacity (100,100 hours/month).
* **Operational Ceiling:** Available R&D capacity supports a maximum of **two major platform integrations concurrently** (absorbing up to 53,900 development hours/month). Attempting a third major integration before standardizing Domo’s continuous delivery pipeline will overflow engineering bandwidth, creating technical debt and delaying feature delivery.

#### 3. Customer Success & SLA Account Capacity
Managing customer success across ~42,400 active enterprise accounts with a streamlined support organization (~450 customer success FTEs) establishes an account-to-FTE ratio of **~94.2 accounts per support FTE**.

* **Escalation SLA Capacity:** At an average enterprise escalation rate of 0.15 critical tickets per account per month, total monthly ticket volume equals ~6,360 tickets. The team’s maximum resolution throughput is ~14.1 tickets per FTE per month.
* **Operational Ceiling:** If ticket volume spikes by **>18%** during post-merger client onboarding or platform consolidation, queue buildup will cause response times to breach enterprise Service Level Agreements (SLAs), driving customer churn above historical baseline levels (8%).
